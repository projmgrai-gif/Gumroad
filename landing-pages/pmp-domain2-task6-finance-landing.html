Section 4 · Q9 Domain II, Task 6

A product owner is deciding whether to pull a high-value feature forward in the backlog. The feature has been delayed twice already, and each additional sprint of delay has a real, estimable cost to the business in lost value. What financial concept does this situation illustrate, and why might it matter to anticipating finance challenges?

ASunk cost — the money already spent on the feature, which should be the deciding factor
BCost of delay — the ongoing cost of NOT delivering a feature, which grows the longer a valuable item sits unaddressed; anticipating that a delay carries a real, growing cost is itself a finance challenge worth factoring into prioritization
CCost of delay applies only to predictive projects, never to backlog-based work
DThe feature's cost of delay is irrelevant to financial planning, since it hasn't been built yet
Show full rationale

Answer: B — Cost of delay captures the real, growing cost of NOT delivering a valuable item, which makes delay itself a financial consideration worth anticipating rather than only a scheduling one — sunk cost looks backward at money already spent and isn't the deciding factor for what to do next (contradicting A), cost of delay is a general prioritization concept, not limited to predictive projects (contradicting C), and a feature not yet built can still carry a real, estimable cost of delay (contradicting D).
